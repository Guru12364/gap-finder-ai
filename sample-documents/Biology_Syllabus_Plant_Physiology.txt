SECTION 1.1: PHOTOSYNTHESIS OVERVIEW & CHLOROPLAST ULTRASTRUCTURE
Photosynthesis is an anabolic, endergonic process whereby green plants synthesize hexose sugars from carbon dioxide and water utilizing solar photons trapped by chlorophyll pigments.
Equation: 6CO2 + 12H2O --[Light & Chlorophyll]--> C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O.
Site: Chloroplast organelle. Thylakoid membranes contain Photosystems I and II (site of light reactions). The fluid stroma contains soluble enzymes for carbon fixation (site of dark reactions / Calvin cycle).
Key Concepts: Chlorophyll a (primary pigment, absorption at 430nm and 660nm), Chlorophyll b, Carotenoids (accessory pigments protecting against photo-oxidation).

SECTION 1.2: LIGHT-DEPENDENT REACTIONS & PHOTOPHOSPHORYLATION
Occurs strictly on the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts.
1. Photolysis of Water: Located at the Oxygen Evolving Complex (OEC) of Photosystem II (P680). 2H2O -> 4H+ + 4e- + O2. Water supplies replacement electrons for PS II and releases oxygen as a byproduct. Requires Mn2+ and Cl- ions.
2. Z-Scheme Electron Flow: Electrons move from PS II -> Plastoquinone (PQ) -> Cytochrome b6f complex -> Plastocyanin (PC) -> Photosystem I (P700).
3. Non-Cyclic Photophosphorylation: Electrons from PS I reduce Ferredoxin (Fd), and Ferredoxin-NADP+ Reductase (FNR) produces NADPH in the stroma.
4. Chemiosmotic ATP Synthesis: Protons (H+) accumulate inside the thylakoid lumen from water splitting and PQ proton pumping. This steep electrochemical gradient drives protons through the CF0-CF1 ATP synthase complex into the stroma, generating ATP.
Key Formulas: 2H2O -> 4H+ + 4e- + O2; Electrochemical delta-pH ~3.0 across thylakoid membrane.

SECTION 1.3: CARBON FIXATION & CALVIN-BENSON CYCLE (DARK REACTIONS)
Occurs in the chloroplast stroma. Does not directly require photons, but relies on ATP and NADPH generated in light reactions.
Phase 1: Carbon Fixation. RuBisCO (Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase) attaches CO2 to 5-carbon RuBP, forming an unstable 6C intermediate that immediately splits into two 3-carbon molecules of 3-Phosphoglycerate (3-PGA).
Phase 2: Reduction. 3-PGA is phosphorylated by ATP and reduced by NADPH to Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P). For every 6 turns of the cycle, 2 net molecules of G3P exit to form one glucose molecule.
Phase 3: Regeneration of RuBP. The remaining 10 G3P molecules are rearranged using 6 ATP molecules to regenerate 6 molecules of RuBP, enabling the cycle to continue.
Key Accounting: Synthesis of 1 Glucose molecule consumes 6 CO2 + 18 ATP + 12 NADPH.

SECTION 1.4: CELLULAR RESPIRATION IN PLANTS
Catabolic oxidation of hexose sugars yielding metabolic energy (ATP).
Glycolysis (cytosol, anaerobic): 1 Glucose -> 2 Pyruvate + 2 net ATP + 2 NADH.
Link Reaction & Krebs Cycle (mitochondrial matrix): Generates NADH, FADH2, and GTP.
Electron Transport Chain & Oxidative Phosphorylation (mitochondrial inner membrane cristae): Oxygen acts as terminal electron acceptor, forming H2O. Total theoretical yield ~30-32 ATP.
Key Concepts: Respiratory Quotient (RQ) = CO2 evolved / O2 consumed. Carbohydrates RQ = 1.0; Fats RQ = 0.7; Organic acids RQ > 1.0.

SECTION 1.5: TRANSPIRATION & STOMATAL MECHANICS
Evaporative loss of water vapor from leaf stomata into the atmosphere.
Cohesion-Tension Theory (Dixon & Joly, 1894): Transpirational pull creates negative hydrostatic pressure (tension) in leaf mesophyll cells. Water columns remain continuous in xylem vessels due to high tensile cohesion (hydrogen bonding between water molecules) and adhesion (attraction to xylem tracheary elements).
Stomatal Mechanism: Active inward pumping of Potassium (K+) and chloride ions into guard cells causes osmotic endosmosis of water. Increased turgor pressure causes the differential expansion of thin outer walls, pulling open the stomatal aperture.
Key Concepts: Transpiration-Photosynthesis compromise; Stomatal aperture regulation.
